ME/CFS and Long COVID

A pacing app for ME/CFS and Long COVID

With ME/CFS (myalgic encephalomyelitis, or chronic fatigue syndrome), and with a lot of Long COVID, you often don't find out you've done too much until a day or two later. Gentle is a free pacing app built around that delay, to help you stay inside your energy envelope and spot a crash coming.

What PEM is, and why it makes pacing hard

Post-exertional malaise, or PEM, is when symptoms get worse after physical, mental or emotional effort. It's the defining feature of ME/CFS, and its timing is what makes it so hard to manage. In a 2018 survey of 150 people with ME/CFS by Chu and colleagues, published in PLoS ONE, most said their PEM lasted 24 hours or more, and some reliably felt it only a day or more after the effort.

So how you feel at the time isn't a reliable guide. The UK's NICE guideline for ME/CFS (NG206, 2021) recommends energy management, which means working out your own energy limits and staying within them, and says graded exercise therapy shouldn't be offered. Gentle works the same way. It never sets you a target to do more, and the step allowance is a ceiling.

Long COVID can bring the same delayed crashes after effort, which is why pacing comes up for both.

Your energy envelope, as a number

The energy envelope is the amount you can do without setting off PEM. It's hard to judge by feel, so Gentle turns it into points. Your ordinary day is 100 points, worked out from your own last 30 days, and each morning Gentle sets today's allowance from your sleep, your heart rate and HRV from your watch, and how you say you feel.

Steps, time with your heart rate over your pace line, and thinking, talking and noise all come off it through the day, so you can see how much is left before you hit the edge. How the points work goes through the sums.

A PEM and crash tracker that warns you early

If the last two days ran well over your ordinary day, this morning's stability score drops, and today's allowance drops with it.

The Today screen shows a PEM risk score out of 100, based on how hard the last three days were, with the reasons listed underneath.

After three big days with the days after them logged, Gentle works out whether you usually feel it the next day or two days later, and warns you on the day it's likely to hit.

Once you've logged three crashes, it looks for what usually came before them and tells you when the same signs show up together. And on a day with a crash or PEM, the allowance goes down by 20% for the whole day, to protect your recovery. The crash and PEM tracker has the details.

Heart-rate pacing with ME/CFS

Gentle sets a pace line from your own resting heart rate (plus 15 beats a minute to start with, or a number your doctor or physio gives you). When you've been over it for most of a stretch of time, your phone tells you how long, like "12 of the last 15 minutes", and the optional watch app buzzes on your wrist. It works with most smartwatches through Health Connect, and with Bluetooth heart-rate armbands. Heart-rate pacing explains how the line and the alerts work.

Pacing with severe ME/CFS

If you're housebound or bed-bound, a pacing app that needs 15 minutes of logging a day is no use, so Gentle asks for very little. The morning check-in is three taps, and every other check-in is optional. You can also say it instead: "Barely slept, really foggy, staying in bed" fills in a check-in, and that works offline with no AI involved.

When you set Gentle up, you tell it how much your health limits you, from mild to very severe, and the starting figures and rest suggestions follow from that. Low-stimulus mode makes everything very dim and still, for light sensitivity or a migraine, and Settings has larger text and a reduce-motion option.

For appointments, Gentle makes a PDF report with charts of fatigue, symptoms, steps, sleep and heart rate, and the crashes you've logged, plus spreadsheets of everything.

Questions about ME/CFS and Long COVID

Is there a free pacing app for ME/CFS?

Gentle is free, with no subscription and no premium version. Every feature, including the PEM tracker, heart-rate alerts and the report for your doctor, is in the free app. It's on Android, and you can download it here until it's on Google Play.

How can I track PEM?

Log a crash or PEM in Gentle when it happens, or afterwards, with how bad it is and what you think set it off. Gentle adds your watch data and check-ins from the days before, then shows a PEM risk score each day, learns when your payback usually arrives, and warns you when the signs that came before your past crashes line up again.

Will Gentle push me to do more exercise?

It won't. There are no goals to beat and no streaks. The step allowance comes from what you've managed on your good days, the ones that weren't followed by a crash, and it's there so you know when to stop. If you log exercise, Gentle counts its cost like anything else.

Can an app predict an ME/CFS crash?

Sometimes, once it has seen a few of yours. Gentle's early crash warning shows what usually came before your crashes, such as a big day, lower HRV or shorter sleep, and how often days like that were followed by one. It's a pattern in your data, so treat it as a warning and not a certainty.

What heart rate should I stay under with ME/CFS?

That's best worked out with your doctor, physio or ME/CFS service. Gentle starts your pace line at your usual resting heart rate plus 15, because that's easy to work out from your own data, and you can change it to 55% of your estimated maximum or any number you're given.

Is Gentle suitable for severe ME/CFS?

It's designed to be. Choose "Severe" or "Very severe" when you set it up, and the starting allowance, step figures and rest advice follow. You don't have to log anything beyond what your watch sends, and you can use a voice note instead of typing.

Does it work with a Polar Verity Sense armband?

Yes. Gentle can connect to Bluetooth heart-rate straps and armbands that use the standard heart-rate signal, which the Polar Verity Sense does. That gives a reading every second and an alert within a minute of going over your line.

Does Gentle replace my doctor's advice?

It doesn't, and it isn't a medical device. What it does is show your own patterns, which gives you and your doctor something concrete to look at.
