How it works

Energy points, worked out from your own days

Pacing means staying within the energy you actually have, so that one good day doesn't cost you three bad ones. The hard part is knowing where your limit is. Gentle gives you a number for it every morning and keeps count for you during the day.

100 points is your ordinary day

That's your ordinary day, not a healthy person's, and it isn't a target either. Gentle looks at your last 30 days, skips crash days and days you barely wore your watch, and takes the middle of what's left as 100 points. Until it has three days of your data, it uses starting figures based on how much your health limits you, and tells you it's doing that.

Those 100 points are split into three parts:

PartWorthMeasured by
Moving about50 pointsSteps, compared with your ordinary day's steps. If you get around seated, active minutes instead.
Heart-rate strain25 pointsMinutes with your heart rate over your pace line, compared with your usual.
Thinking, social and sensory25 pointsThe effort you rate in the evening check-in, and work shifts. Until you've rated it, an ordinary amount for the time you've been up.

Each part is measured against what's usual for you, so a quiet day comes out under 100 and a busy one over. Twice your usual steps adds roughly 50 points. A noisy family meal shows up in the third part, even if you sat down the whole time.

Today's allowance

Each day starts from 100 and is multiplied by whatever is going on today, and Gentle shows you the sum. For the sample day in the screenshot, it's:

100 × 1.10 × 0.90 = 99 points

The 1.10 is for a stable morning, and the 0.90 is because the sample person has had more crash days than usual lately. These are the things that can change it:

  • How stable your morning is, scored from 1 to 5. That comes from last night's sleep, your overnight HRV and resting heart rate compared with usual, yesterday's symptoms, how tired you say you are, and whether you've overdone it in the last two days. A 5 multiplies the allowance by 1.10, a 3 by 0.90 and a 1 by 0.55.
  • A crash or PEM today takes 20% off for the whole day.
  • An any-time check-in can move the allowance part of the way toward how you say you feel. Saying you feel about normal, or better, never lowers it.
  • Symptoms that are worse than usual for you take 5% off if they're moderate and 10% if severe, up to 15% in total. Mild ones don't count.
  • If you've had more crash days than is normal for you over the last few weeks, it goes down. After a calmer spell Gentle offers a 10% rise, but only makes it if you say yes.
  • If one part of your menstrual cycle turns out to be harder for you, those days are 10% lower.

It never drops below 35 points, so there's always enough for the basics.

Planning a day

In Plan my day you can put activities into the morning, afternoon or evening and see what they'll cost before you start. The cost depends on how long something takes and whether you're standing, sitting or lying down. For the sample person, a 15-minute shower costs 6 points, cooking a full meal 18, and a 15-minute phone call 3.

Gentle suggests planning around 80% of your allowance, which leaves room for surprises. Rest goes in the plan as well, and each block comes with a suggested rest to suit how much your health limits you.

If you use the activity timer while wearing your watch, Gentle compares what your heart rate did with its estimate. After about three goes it switches to what it measured.

It learns what a busy day costs you

Charging every step at the same rate doesn't suit everyone. Some people walk all day at work and feel fine, while for others a trip to the shops means losing the weekend. So Gentle keeps an eye on your big step days (at least half as many steps again as usual) and on what happens after them.

Suppose those days are followed by a crash, or by feeling noticeably worse in the next two days, no more often than your other days are. Then steps beyond your ordinary day start to cost less, down to half price at most, and only up to the most steps you've done without paying for it later. If big days do catch up with you, they stay at full price. Gentle needs four big days, each with a check-in in the two days after, before it changes anything.

You can also tell Gentle your ordinary day has more steps than it thinks, by saying something like "I usually do about 6,000 steps".

Boom and bust

A lot of pacing advice is aimed at one familiar pattern. You feel all right, so you catch up on everything, then you pay for it with days of doing nothing, and as soon as you feel all right again it starts over. Gentle looks out for it. When big days keep being followed by crashes, the Today screen tells you how many times it's happened in the last four weeks and suggests spreading things out more evenly.

The crash log goes further and learns which signs tend to come before your crashes.

Questions about the points

Is this the same as spoon theory?

Yes, it's the same idea. In Christine Miserandino's spoon theory, you start each day with a set number of spoons and everything you do uses some. Gentle's points work like that, except the number you start with changes with your morning, and the costs come from your own data.

Why did my allowance go down after a check-in?

It only goes down if you said you feel worse than on an ordinary day, or ticked a moderate or severe symptom that's worse than usual for you. If you say you feel about normal or better, it stays where the morning put it. Tap "How was this worked out?" to see which line changed.

When do my points reset?

At the time you set under "My day ends at", anywhere from midnight to 6am. It starts at 5am, so a late night still counts toward the day you were living.

Does editing the budget change the points I've already used?

It doesn't. Changing the allowance changes the total for the day, and anything you've already used stays as it is.

I use a wheelchair. Do steps still count?

Turn on "I mostly get around seated" in Settings, and Gentle uses active minutes and heart rate instead of steps.

Can I set my own numbers?

Yes. You can set your ordinary day's steps, your sustainable steps and your own pace line, and change what any planned activity costs.
