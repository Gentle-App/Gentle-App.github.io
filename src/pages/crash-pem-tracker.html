Crashes and PEM

A crash log that looks for what came first

Logging a crash takes a few seconds, and you can do it afterwards. The useful part comes later, when Gentle looks back at the days before each crash and finds what they had in common.

Logging one

Go to Log, then "Log a crash, PEM or dip", or just say it: "I crashed this afternoon after the hospital appointment." Gentle picks out when it started, that it's still going and what you think set it off, and shows you before saving.

There are four kinds you can log. An energy dip is a temporary drop that usually eases with rest. A crash is a bigger setback, where you have to stop and rest for a day or more. PEM is when symptoms get worse after physical, mental or emotional effort, often 12 to 72 hours later. And a symptom flare is when one or more symptoms suddenly get worse.

Each one can be happening now or have happened earlier, and can be ongoing or over, with a severity and a trigger if you know it. When it eases, tap "It's easing now" on the Today screen, or answer the question in your morning check-in.

What Gentle learns from your crashes

What usually comes first

Once you've logged three crashes with a few days of data before each, the early crash warning compares those days with your usual ones. It might find that your crashes tend to follow a day over 120% of your ordinary day, a night of shorter sleep and lower HRV, for example. Then it shows how often days like that were followed by a crash, which for the sample person in the screenshots was 3 times out of 17. When those signs line up again, the Today screen shows an early warning.

When payback arrives

After three big days with the days after them logged, Gentle works out whether you usually feel it the next day or the day after, and warns you on that day.

Whether you're crashing more than usual

If you've had more crash days than is normal for you over the last few weeks, Gentle lowers your allowance a little until things settle. After a long calm spell it offers a 10% rise, and only makes it if you agree.

Words in your notes

If the same word keeps turning up in your notes in the days before a crash, like "visitors" or "drive", Gentle points it out.

These are patterns in your own data, and they don't prove what caused a crash. They get more reliable the more you log, and you can switch each one off in Settings.

On the day itself

While a crash or PEM is logged for today, your allowance is 20% lower for the whole day, so the app isn't nudging you to use energy you don't have. If one that started the night before is over by your morning check-in, it counts for the day it began, and today counts as the day after a crash. That still lowers your morning stability, but doesn't take another 20% off.

Every crash also goes into the report for your doctor, with the dates, how long it lasted and how bad it was.

Questions about the crash log

I forgot to log a crash. Can I add it later?

Yes. Pick when it started, whether it's still going and when it ended. You can also mark it in that day's evening check-in.

How many crashes before the early warning works?

Three, each with some data from the days before. Until then, the "What Gentle has learned" screen tells you how many more it needs.

Can I see all my past crashes?

Go to Log, then Crash log. Each one shows how long it lasted, how bad it was and what you thought set it off. History lets you search back through everything else.

What's the difference between a crash and PEM?

In Gentle, a crash is any setback where you have to stop and rest for a day or more. PEM means symptoms getting worse after effort, often a day or two later. Both lower your allowance the same way, so log whichever fits.
