Heart-rate pacing

A pace line from your own heart rate

Heart-rate pacing is easy to describe and hard to do. You keep your heart rate under a line, and when it goes over, you stop and rest. Gentle works out the line for you, counts the time you spend above it, and tells you when you've been over for a while.

Where the line comes from

Your pace line starts at your usual resting heart rate plus 15 beats a minute. "Usual" here means the middle of your last 30 days, leaving out crash, PEM and infection days, because those push resting heart rate up and would drag the line up with it. So if your resting heart rate is usually 64, your line is 79.

You can pick a different method in Settings:

  • resting heart rate plus 15 (the default)
  • 55% of your estimated maximum, which is 220 minus your age
  • a number of your own, for example one your doctor or physio gave you

Gentle can't tell you what heart rate is safe for you. The line is a starting point based on your resting heart rate, and if it feels too high or too low, you can change it.

What counts as time over the line

Heart rate on the wrist jumps about, and one spike when you stand up shouldn't cost you anything. So Gentle smooths the readings minute by minute and only counts stretches of three minutes or more that are at least 2 beats over the line. Those minutes make up a quarter of your energy points, measured against how much time you usually spend over the line.

If your watch shows a very high heart rate while you were barely moving, Gentle asks whether it was real before counting it. A watch that's slipped on your wrist can read 190 while you're lying on the sofa.

Alerts that say how long

Each time new heart rate comes in, Gentle looks back from the newest reading over the last 15, 30, 45, 60, 90 and 120 minutes. If you've been over your line for at least two-thirds of any of those, your phone tells you, using whichever stretch had the most time over:

  • "Over 79 bpm for 12 of the last 15 minutes" early on
  • "42 of the last 45 minutes" if you've kept going
  • "40 of the last 60 minutes" if it's been on and off

It only sends one if you're still over the line in the last few minutes, and never more than once every half hour. Alerts follow your day, from when you get up until your usual bedtime, which Gentle works out from your sleep. If you're up until 2am, so are the alerts.

Tap the alert for some ways to bring your heart rate down, like sitting or lying down with your legs up and breathing out slowly.

Three ways to get heart rate into Gentle

Health Connect

This works with most watches, but the timing can lag. Apps like Google Health pass readings on in batches, sometimes an hour or two late. Gentle tells you when that's happening and offers to open the app so it syncs.

The Gentle watch app

For Wear OS watches such as Pixel Watch. It sends heart rate every few minutes, buzzes on your wrist when a reading is over your line (at most every 5 minutes), and shows your points left. If the watch restarts, it starts sending again by itself.

A Bluetooth strap

A chest strap or armband gives a reading every second, so Gentle can alert you within a minute of going over. It's the quickest of the three, though not the comfiest thing to wear all day.

Questions about heart-rate pacing

What heart rate should I pace at?

That's best worked out with your doctor, physio or specialist service, because Gentle can't know what's right for you. It starts at your usual resting heart rate plus 15 because that's easy to work out from your own data, and you can type in any number they suggest.

Why didn't I get an alert?

There are a few possible reasons. You might not have been over the line for two-thirds of any of the time windows, or you came back under it in the last few minutes. It might be outside your waking hours, or less than half an hour since the last alert. Or your watch's app hasn't passed the readings on yet. You can switch pace alerts off and on in Settings.

Does my line change when I'm in a crash?

It doesn't, unless you'd like it to. The line is set from calm days only, so a flare doesn't move it. If you'd rather it followed your last two weeks, flares included, there's a switch for that.

Do I need the Gentle watch app?

It's optional. Heart rate through Health Connect is enough for the points and the phone alerts. The watch app makes alerts quicker and adds a buzz on your wrist.

Free, and it'll stay free

There will never be a subscription or a premium version. The app has small banner ads, which a one-off 99p (99 cents in the US) gets rid of, and that's the only thing you can buy. Your health data stays on your phone, and nobody else gets it, me included.
